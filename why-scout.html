Scout

Built while building a business.

Harbor started because we were struggling to keep up with the growth of our own business.

As CSC grew, we found ourselves spending more and more time managing information instead of serving customers.

Every order created a trail of emails, phone calls, supplier conversations, quotes, tracking numbers, notes, and follow-up tasks. A customer would contact us looking for a specific part, and we would immediately begin reaching out to multiple suppliers to compare pricing and availability.

In other cases, we would sell a product and then start coordinating with suppliers to fulfill the order and ensure it shipped on time.

The work itself wasn’t the problem.

The problem was everything surrounding the work.

Before long, it felt like we were trying to play a clean game of telephone — digitally.

Information was constantly being passed from customer to supplier, supplier to employee, employee to spreadsheet, spreadsheet to email, and back again. Every handoff created another opportunity for something to be missed, delayed, or forgotten.

It wasn’t that the information didn’t exist.

It was that the information lived everywhere.

Some of it was in our inboxes. Some of it was in spreadsheets. Some of it was in notes. Some of it was in supplier portals. Some of it lived only in somebody’s memory.

As the business grew, keeping everyone aligned became exhausting.

We were constantly switching between systems trying to answer what should have been simple questions.

What did we quote? Who is waiting on a response? Which supplier has the best availability? Did the order ship? Did someone follow up with the customer?

What started as a manageable process slowly became harder and harder to maintain.

And the reality is, we don’t think we’re alone.

Most growing businesses aren’t struggling because they lack effort. They’re struggling because information becomes fragmented as they grow.

Every new customer, supplier, project, asset, document, and workflow introduces another place where information can get lost.

Eventually, you spend more time searching for information than acting on it.

That’s the moment Harbor was born.

We started building a system for ourselves. A place where customers, suppliers, orders, assets, documents, requests, projects, and conversations could finally live together.

A place where information remained connected. A place where everyone could see what was happening without digging through inboxes, spreadsheets, and disconnected systems.

We didn’t build Harbor because we wanted another piece of software.

We built Harbor because we wanted less software.

Less jumping between tabs. Less duplicate data entry. Less searching. Less confusion. Less time spent trying to remember where something lives.

The name Harbor came naturally.

A harbor is a place where things come together. A place of protection. A place of organization. A place that helps you stay on course when things around you become busy and unpredictable.

That’s what we wanted Harbor to become for growing businesses.

Today, Harbor is still guided by that same idea.

We’re building the platform we wish we had while scaling CSC.

A place where information has a home. A place where teams stay aligned. A place where businesses can focus less on managing information and more on serving customers.

Because growing a business is hard enough.

Keeping it organized shouldn’t be.

Jeff Short
Founder, Harbor
