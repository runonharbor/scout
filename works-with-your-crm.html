Two different kinds of questions

Great CRMs manage customer data.

CRMs help companies understand their pipeline, forecast revenue, maintain customer records, and give leadership visibility into the business.

Those are important questions.

But they aren't usually the questions an outside sales rep is asking when the day begins.

1

How many opportunities are currently in the pipeline?

2

What is the sales forecast for this month or quarter?

3

What stage is each deal currently in?

4

Which accounts and opportunities need attention?

5

What does leadership need to know?

The questions reps ask

Outside sales happens somewhere else.

Reps aren't only thinking about reports and dashboards. They're trying to decide where to go, who to contact, what to remember, and how to keep every opportunity moving forward.

1

Who should I stop and see today?

2

What did we talk about the last time I was there?

3

Did I ever send that customer the quote I promised?

4

Who said they would call me back?

5

Which customers haven't heard from me recently?

6

What opportunities am I closest to winning?

7

Where should I spend my time today?

8

What small step can I take to move this relationship forward?

Those aren't reporting questions.

Those are selling questions. That's where Scout comes in.

Outside sales is different

We didn't build Scout because Salesforce wasn't good enough.

We built Scout because outside sales happens on the road, between appointments, on job sites, during phone calls, across a desk, and inside dozens of small conversations.

Those moments deserve software built specifically for the people doing the work.

On the road

Planning visits and deciding where your time matters most.

On job sites

Capturing details while conversations are still fresh.

During phone calls

Remembering promises, questions, and the next follow-up.

Between appointments

Turning handwritten notes into clear next steps.
